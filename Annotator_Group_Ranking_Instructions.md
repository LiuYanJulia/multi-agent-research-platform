# Annotator Instructions for Group-Based Creativity Ranking

Today, you will evaluate a set of design proposals for an adult learning platform.

You will evaluate the proposals in small groups. Each group contains three proposals. For each group, please follow this process:

1. First, read all three proposals in the group before assigning any scores. While reading, you may make brief notes about what seems novel, useful, appropriate, or underdeveloped.

2. Second, after reading all three proposals, evaluate each proposal individually using the creativity evaluation form. This is the most important part of the annotation. For each proposal, please assign scores for all 10 evaluation items as accurately as possible, based on the criteria provided in the form.

The scores should be relative to the broader set of proposals you have evaluated so far in this session, not only relative to the other two proposals in the current group. In other words, when you assign a score from 1 to 7, please consider how this proposal compares with all previous proposals you have already evaluated. As you see more proposals, please keep using the same overall scale and calibrate your scores against the full range of proposals seen so far.

Please try to judge the proposal itself, not the participant's writing style, English fluency, or length of the response.

3. Third, after you have scored all three proposals in the current group, compare the three proposals with each other. Please identify:
   - the most creative proposal in this group,
   - the middle proposal,
   - the least creative proposal.

If two proposals are genuinely too close to distinguish, you may indicate a tie, but please use ties only when necessary.

When making this ranking, please compare only the three proposals in the current group. This group ranking is a secondary record of the relative order within the current group.

For example, one proposal may be the most creative in the current group, but if it still feels relatively conventional compared with earlier proposals, you can rank it first within the group while giving it a moderate numerical creativity score. Similarly, if all three proposals in a group are strong compared with previous proposals, they can all receive relatively high scores, even though you still rank them as most creative, middle, and least creative within the group.

Finally, please write a brief reason for your group ranking. One or two sentences is enough. You can mention whether the top-ranked proposal seemed more novel, more useful for adult learners, more feasible, more original, or stronger as an overall creative product within the current group.

Please remember that the numerical scores in the creativity evaluation form are the primary data. These scores should reflect your overall expert judgment across all proposals evaluated so far. The group ranking only captures the relative ordering within the current group.

There are no right or wrong answers. We are interested in your expert judgment and reasoning.
